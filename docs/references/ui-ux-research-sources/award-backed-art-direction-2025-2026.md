### Research delta — 2026-11-27: quiet productivity UI and color direction
Public documentary evidence, not a universal 2026 trend census or a product decision.
- Linear's March 12, 2026 UI refresh describes a calmer interface, dimmer navigation and stronger content emphasis: https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-12-ui-refresh and https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh .
- Vercel Geist separates primary/secondary backgrounds from default, hover, active, border and text/icon roles: https://vercel.com/geist/colors .
- Radix distinguishes neutral gray pairing from subtle natural hue pairing and cautions that heavily tinted backgrounds may clash with colorful components: https://www.radix-ui.com/colors/docs/palette-composition/composing-a-palette .
- shadcn/ui documents neutral families and semantic theme tokens: https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/theming .
Design hypothesis: compare neutral monochrome, cool neutral + restrained accent and warm neutral + restrained accent in identical reading-intensive task screens. Separate brand/action/status roles; assess selection and feedback without hue alone, contrast, scanability and preference in both themes. No specific hue is universally modern; no live UI or participant test conducted. ## Reusable evaluation protocol

- Start from task, audience, positioning and reading/operation context, not a favorite hue; separate brand, editorial and daily-use interfaces.
- Compare distinct art directions together with typography, layout rhythm, surface hierarchy, iconography, wordmark and semantic color using identical representative tasks, states and content in both themes. Editorial and instrumental precision are hypotheses, not standards.
- Avoid generic template recolors, indiscriminate accent fills, unnecessary cards, ornamental gradients, excessive radii and decorative iconography when they compete with the task; these are not universal bans.
- Evaluate legibility, scanability, task hierarchy, semantic recognition, contrast, focus, non-color cues, state coverage, layout stability, responsive rendering and distinctiveness. Require real captures with provenance; do not infer CSS or interaction from still images.
- Preserve product scope and stage gates. Awards are not proof of usability or accessibility. Record accepted/rejected alternatives and uncertainty.
